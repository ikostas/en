Life Is Harder, Make No Mistake

  • 22 November 2026
  • #productivity,
  • #job search

Intro

I've read some stories online about young people facing anxiety and disappointment for different reasons, starting from the climate change, conflicts, future uncertainty in general and ending with a frustrating job search experiences. My personal take on this: life did become harder in many aspects, and you are not exaggerating, overreacting or hallucinating. What's happening is the race for power and resources, and it's not humane in any way. I've seen people successfully evading reality, and there are a lot of places in this world where you can do that without any substances, where history has just stopped, but I don't think it's the way out. My answer is to face reality and deal with it. And this post is a short description of how I do it.

Wars

The war in Ukraine affected me directly, so I heard a lot of opinions from Russians and Ukrainians, as well as listened to analytical podcasts trying to point out the reasons for what's going on. And there was one phrase that made it clear to me: soldiers pay for the mistakes of politicians. As I understand it, not being a politician myself, the whole point of politics is to prevent wars and to build a peace architecture. So you have wars when politics and politicians have failed. And when you unleash the war dogs, you don't manage the situation anymore, you expect the outcome.

Next, I believe humans can deal with a finite amount of trauma, including seeing the traumatic situations of other people, so I don't think looking away helps, but knowing your limits definitely does.

You also need to understand how news agencies have worked for centuries: the more trash they bring to the attention of listeners, viewers and readers, the larger the audience they get. If you read 100-year-old newspapers, it's about the end of the world and humans experiencing suffering. Bringing attention to these issues is necessary to resolve them, but drama doesn't help the people who actually work on that (ask me how I know).

The Middle East has had different types of conflicts; they are centuries old, and life is not enough to study even one of them in its complexity. But life is enough to watch the "Fauda" series, and it's definitely worth it.

It's about understanding your limits, what you can face, understand or change. And I think doing something locally to the best of your abilities is the best way to change the world in the best possible way, but there is a catch. Being in a war zone isn't for civilians, so if you are there, just get out first.

Structural crises

There's a structural crisis going on, probably several of them simultaneously. Mixing them with other unfortunate events doesn't help, but increases anxiety. By structural crisis, I mean the IT/AI crisis, but I believe that every crisis opens new opportunities. Some old opportunities are closing, and being stubborn and following the old path doesn't help.

Let's look at it from another angle: if you are in a startup environment, you'll hear the word "disruptive" in a positive sense. The new companies should disrupt old businesses by bringing substantially more value and offering cheaper goods and services. It's nice to use the fruits of these disruptive changes, but living in this time is rough, especially if your job is the one being made obsolete.

And let's go one step further: I've seen businesses, business models or even industries that should be disrupted. They look disgusting inside because they don't care about the value they bring, they do not know who their customers are and they have forgotten about costs completely. Sometimes this can't be cured, and it takes more time to go bankrupt when the company has a pile of cash.

Let's get to the measures to live with the crises:

  • Adapt, there's no way around it.
  • Choose between official courses and self-study consciously, because "official" doesn't guarantee anything.
  • Move faster, experiment, check the facts.

Jobs

Job search takes its toll on everyone: depreciated effort, ego being humiliated, dealing with incompetence and discrimination. The question is even how to get to the people who are still able to listen and choose reasonably. I assume that everyone gets a chance by putting enough effort into it. But let's assume you just finished a credible institute and you have to compete with a 40-50-year-old fart like me. Let's imagine you are sitting in the same room and you have a chance to persuade the employer to choose you – what will you say?

  • Working more? What I've seen, people providing for a family don't lack motivation.
  • Learning faster? Some people are stuck, but don't underestimate others; people can learn.
  • Being friendlier or playing the "work relations" card in some other way? I've seen a lot of people doing it.

I think you need a plan. Some particular points of it could be the following:

  • Taking a job in a remote location; the competition for these jobs can be dramatically lower, and you'll get the experience you need. Married people are usually tied to places.
  • Having a particular suggestions to substitute the old tech stack. People are afraid to have suggestions, and a person with a wrong suggestion can still win over a person with no suggestions.
  • Digging deeper into the basics: business, process modeling, anything. I've seen a lot of people who have only superficial knowledge, and they are not ready for a discussion. And the management knows it; they just don't have anyone to choose from.

Inputs

Our minds and bodies run the processes, and only the processes are important at the end of the day. You've probably heard of "Atomic Habits", and that's what forms the processes. What I mean by processes is that our minds and bodies transform inputs into outputs. It's extremely important to watch the inputs:

  • What food we digest
  • What information you consume
  • How much and how well you sleep; it's an input as well, and it helps your mind and body to recover
  • Do you challenge your mind with tasks?
  • Do you stimulate your body with physical exercises?
  • Do you have spiritual development?

The Greeks, in their wisdom, invented and described all this long ago, as well as critical thinking and rules of discussion. And these basics still allow people to win. It's now just packaged as energy management or mood management. I was surprised how important it actually is for me to listen to the good old songs in the morning and how important the preparation-for-sleep routine turned out to be. And I read exactly one source of information regarding politics, just to stay sane.

Conclusions

With my parents, I remember the exact point in time when I realized that their life experience was no longer relevant and I shouldn't listen to their advice. Maybe my advice is outdated as well – if so, I'd vote for critical analysis of the information in this post above everything else.

This moment was liberating in some ways, as well as making me feel the full load of responsibility for my life and the lives of those depending on me. What I regret most is that, with the current turbulence, boys and girls are experiencing this moment when they are not ready yet; they are not tough enough. The psyche can crack under this load, and you can see it's happening almost online. And those who choose violent protests are not going to listen; they believe that there's an easy fix.

Anyway, everything's gonna be alright. ©
